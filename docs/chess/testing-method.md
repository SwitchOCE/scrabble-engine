## Summary

- **Every Stockfish change is a sequential test against master:** first a short time control (STC 10+0.1), then
  a long one (LTC 60+0.6). Each test stops when the log-likelihood ratio crosses ±2.94, which means α = β = 0.05.
  Simplifications get "non-regression" bounds instead, and about as many commits pass those as pass gainer
  bounds (1,158 vs 1,254).
- **The bounds moved from BayesElo (to 2020-01) to logistic (2020) to normalized Elo (from 2021-04-25).** In
  normalized Elo, a test's length depends only on its bounds, not on the draw rate or the opening book.
- **Games are played in pairs:** the same opening, colours swapped. Results are scored per pair (pentanomial).
  In one LTC test the pentanomial variance per game is 0.47 of the trinomial one, so pairing about halves the
  games needed.
- **For Scrabble, paired bags are the game pairs.** With our 6 cores, an SPRT with a 2-percentage-point window
  (about 14 Elo) costs roughly 900–1,750 pairs in the worst case if pairing gains 1.5–3× (2,709 with no gain).
  A patch-vs-master test is sim against sim, so that is about 0.8–2.4 days at 2-ply and 1.8–5.4 days at 3-ply.
  Effects of 1 Elo, the size of recent leave work, are out of reach of SPRT on this machine.
- **Macondo autoplay already has paired bags and paired statistics.** It lacks SPRT stopping, a pentanomial
  tally, equal-time sims that still replay exactly, non-regression mode and a test queue.

## 1. SPRT and the bounds

### Facts

- **The test.** Fishtest runs a generalised SPRT (GSPRT): unknown parameters in H0 and H1 are replaced by
  maximum-likelihood estimates (fishtest wiki, "Fishtest mathematics"). Commit messages print `LLR: 2.94
  (-2.94,2.94)`, and ln(19) = 2.944 is the bound for α = β = 0.05 (Van den Bergh, §3, `b = log(19)`). A test
  passes at +2.94, fails at −2.94, and should not be stopped by hand ("Creating my first test").
- **The Elo model of the bounds**, from the parser over 4,681 SPRTs with bounds (stockfish-commit-stats.md,
  "Field coverage"; detail in data/sprt-bounds-summary.txt):

| Model | Period in messages | Typical gainer STC | Typical gainer LTC | Typical non-regression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BayesElo | 2013-09 to 2020-01 | [−1.5, 4.5] (2013–2020), [0, 5] (2015–2018), [0, 4] (2014–2019), [0.5, 4.5] (2019) | [0, 6] (2013–2015), [0, 5], [0, 4], [0, 3.5] (2019) | [−3, 1] (2014–2020) |
| logistic | 2020-01-07 to 2021-04-28 | {−0.5, 1.5}, then {−0.25, 1.25} (from 2020-08) | {0.25, 1.75}, then {0.25, 1.25} | {−1.5, 0.5}, then {−1.25, 0.25} STC / {−0.75, 0.25} LTC |
| normalized | from 2021-04-25 | <−0.5, 2.5> → <0, 2.5> (2021-11) → **<0, 2>** (2022-08 on) | <0.5, 3.5> → <0.5, 3.0> → **<0.5, 2.5>** (2022-08 on) | <−2.5, 0.5> → <−2.25, 0.25> → **<−1.75, 0.25>** (2022-08 on) |

- **Normalized Elo** (Van den Bergh, §2–3). Define:
  - µ = expected score, and σ_pg = standard deviation per game (pentanomial: the SD of the pair score scored
    0, ¼, ½, ¾, 1, times √2);
  - t_n = (µ − ½)/σ_pg, and normalized Elo e_n = C·t_n with C = 800/ln 10 ≈ 347.4.

  For a balanced book, e_n ≈ logistic Elo/√(1 − d), where d is the draw ratio. At d = 0.5, 5 nElo = 3.54
  logistic Elo. At d = 0.9, 5 nElo = 1.58 logistic Elo.
- **Test length.** With α = β = 0.05, the worst-case expected length (true value halfway between the bounds) is
  `T = 1,046,535 / (e_n1 − e_n0)²` games (Van den Bergh, eq. 3.4). A 2-nElo-wide window therefore needs at most
  ~261,600 games on average.
- **Observed lengths** of passed gainer tests: median 96,384 games at STC (p90 327,136) and 109,002 at LTC
  (p90 312,666), 2024–2026. In 2013–2019 the medians were 23,057 and 32,447 (sprt-bounds-summary.txt).

### Inference

- The move to normalized Elo made test cost predictable. Under BayesElo and logistic bounds, the same window
  took longer as books, time controls and draw rates changed. Normalized bounds fix the information needed, not
  the Elo.
- Observed medians are below the worst-case figure because passing patches are often clearly above the upper
  bound, and the worst case assumes the true value sits at the midpoint.
- Test length grew about 4× from the 2010s to now. That is the cost of chasing 1–2 Elo patches (median accepted
  LTC gain 1.5, stockfish-commit-stats.md "Inference").

## 2. Pentanomial statistics and why game pairs matter

### Facts

- Fishtest plays each opening twice with colours reversed and scores the pair as one of five outcomes (LL, LD,
  DD or WL, WD, WW) (fishtest wiki; CPW, "Match Statistics"). CPW: the trinomial model "is not correct in case
  games are played in pairs with reversed colors (as is commonly the case) and unbalanced opening positions are
  used". The trinomial model overestimates the variance, and so the number of games needed.
- In commit messages, `Ptnml(0-2)` counts appear from 2020. None of the 1,776 parsed tests from 2013–2019 has
  them; 502 of 506 in 2020 do, and nearly all after (sprt-bounds-summary.txt). Recent messages also print
  `nElo` and `PairsRatio` (for example af110e02: "nElo: −4.62 … PairsRatio: 0.95").
- **Worked example** (584d9efe "Dual NNUE", passed LTC; sprt-scrabble-calc.txt):
  - Counts: W 16,118, L 15,738, D 32,974 over 64,830 games; pairs 43 / 7,129 / 17,697 / 7,497 / 49.
  - Trinomial σ_pg = 0.350; pentanomial σ_pg = 0.240. The variance ratio is 0.47.
  - So the same precision needs about 47% of the games under the pair model.
  - The same result is 2.04 logistic Elo, 2.91 nElo under the trinomial model and 4.24 nElo under the
    pentanomial one.

### Inference

- Pairs cancel the opening's bias: whatever advantage the opening gives one colour, each engine gets it once.
  What is left is the difference between the engines. Only "lopsided" pairs (WW, LL, WD, LD) carry
  information; a WL pair says the opening decided it.
- That is also why fishtest moved to unbalanced books (next section): with an imbalanced opening, fewer pairs end
  DD, so more pairs are informative.

## 3. Time controls, non-regression tests and books

### Facts

- **The ladder** ("Creating my first test"):
  - STC 10+0.1, then LTC 60+0.6, both 1 thread;
  - SMP tests on 8 threads;
  - "scaler" tests at 180+1.8 (1 thread) or 60+0.6 (8 threads) / 70+0.7 (7 threads).

  Commit messages label the scalers VLTC (for example "VLTC 180+1.8") and VVLTC ("VVLTC 70+0.7 th7"). Hash
  is 16 MB at 20+0.2 and 64 MB at 60+0.6.
- **Patches that pass STC and fail LTC** are "mixed" in the parser (11 commits). Several were merged anyway
  (speedups, a mate fix) (stockfish-commit-stats.md).
- **Speedups are tested at STC only.** 105 speedup gain commits have STC Elo; 17 have LTC Elo.
- **Parameter tunes run at very long TC.** "Tune search at very long time control" gained +15.7 at VLTC
  (84b1940f). Many VVLTC SPSA tunes add +3 to +7 each (stockfish-commit-stats.md, "Largest gains without an LTC
  Elo").
- **Non-regression bounds** <−1.75, 0.25> are used for simplifications that change behaviour, even one-liners.
  Refactors and pre-approved bug fixes also use them ("Creating my first test").
  - In the parser, 614 of 750 simplification commits passed non-regression, and 23 passed gainer bounds.
  - Maintainers sometimes merge a removal that measurably costs Elo, for code size:
    - "Remove classical evaluation" (af110e02) quotes −2.35 STC and −1.74 LTC over 100,000 games, and was
      merged as "roughly 25% of SF";
    - "Remove small net" (ab8f901d) failed STC and VVLTC non-regression and was merged.
- **Books.** Recent messages and local tests use `UHO_Lichess_4852_v1.epd` (for example commits-full.txt lines
  9897–9899, 2110). Older ones name "UHO_XXL_+0.90_+1.19" (by its name, positions evaluated between +0.90 and +1.19). Median
  draw rate in parsed SPRTs at LTC fell from 0.683 (2013–2019) and 0.755 (2020 to 2021-04) to 0.489 (2021-05 to
  2023) and 0.492 (2024–2026). STC fell from about 0.6 to 0.48 (sprt-bounds-summary.txt).

### Inference

- The fall in draw rate around 2021 coincides with the normalized-Elo switch. It is consistent with a move to
  unbalanced ("UHO") books. That book change is UNVERIFIED here: the switch date was not found in the messages.
- **The ladder is a cost filter.** Most ideas die at STC cheaply. LTC catches ideas that do not scale. Long
  scalers are kept for parameters that depend on depth.
- **Non-regression testing is half the process, and it is what keeps the code small enough to keep improving.**

## 4. Applying it to Scrabble

Everything below is **Inference**, built on the facts above and the Scrabble sources cited.

### Paired bags are the game pairs

- Macondo's `-gamepairs` plays each seed twice with the seats swapped. The bag is fixed-order, and each seat
  draws from its own end, so both seatings see the same tiles (reuse-check.md, "Paired bags";
  `game/pairedbag.go:8-34` in macondo `9c33baa`).
- A pair scores 0, ½, 1, 1½ or 2 wins; ties are rare. The pentanomial model applies unchanged.
- **The "draw" of Scrabble pairs is a 1–1 pair.** Each seat won once, so the bag decided the game. Those pairs
  carry no information. The share of 2–0 and 0–2 pairs sets test cost, the way the DD share does in chess. It
  should be logged per run.
- **Pairing gain is not yet measured on our side.**
  - Macondo says pairing is "worth about 3x the games" (`docs/inference_experiments.md:77-81`, quoted in
    macondo.md).
  - Its 100k-pair SE of 0.09 win points (`pytorch/RUNS.md:6-8`), against 0.112 for 200k unpaired games at
    σ = 0.5, implies a variance ratio of about 0.65, or 1.5×.
  - We use r = 1.5–3 below and should measure r on our first baseline.

### Test length on our CPU

Worst-case expected length, in win-share terms: `T_games = ln(19)² · σ_pg² / Δµ²`, with `σ_pg² = 0.25 / r`. This
is the same formula as Van den Bergh's eq. 3.4, rewritten without the Elo constant. Pairs = T/2.

| Window width Δµ | r = 1 (no gain) | r = 1.55 | r = 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.0 pp (≈ 7 Elo) | 10,837 pairs | 6,992 | 3,612 |
| 1.5 pp | 4,817 | 3,107 | 1,606 |
| 2.0 pp (≈ 14 Elo) | 2,709 | 1,748 | 903 |
| 2.5 pp | 1,734 | 1,119 | 578 |
| 3.0 pp | 1,204 | 777 | 401 |

(1 percentage point of win share near 50% ≈ 6.95 logistic Elo.)

Throughput on our 6 cores, from macondo.md ("Autoplay as referee"; sim bot vs the nearly free HastyBot):

- 2-ply: about 2,300 pairs/day;
- 3-ply: about 1,000 pairs/day;
- 4–5-ply: a few hundred pairs/day;
- sim vs sim: about half those rates.

### Proposed ladder and bounds (draft for step 5(c))

This is a draft for the step-5(c) decision, not an adopted method.

| Stage | Chess analogue | Setting | Bounds (paired win share) | Worst-case cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S0: proxy | local fixed-node net selection | Decision-quality checks against a long reference sim (Macondo `decidelab`, `simlabel`-style agreement; macondo.md) on a fixed position set | none (rank candidates) | minutes–hours |
| S1: "STC" | STC 10+0.1 | 2-ply sim, fixed small iteration budget per move (deterministic, so pairs replay exactly), vs a fixed opponent | gainer <0, 2 pp>; non-regression <−1.5, +0.5 pp> | ~900–1,750 pairs at r = 1.5–3 (2,709 at r = 1); well under a day if S1 games cost ≤ ¼ of a full 2-ply game |
| S2: "LTC" | LTC 60+0.6 | 2-ply at full budget, or 3-ply | <0.5, 2.5 pp> | ~900–2,709 pairs (r = 3 to 1); sim vs sim, so ~0.8–2.4 days at 2-ply and ~1.8–5.4 days at 3-ply |
| S3: "scaler" | VLTC | 4–5-ply, equal time per move | fixed-length run with a CI, not SPRT (a few hundred pairs/day) | days |

Notes on the ladder:

- **α = β = 0.05 and LLR bounds ±2.94, like fishtest.** Use one window width (2 pp) at every stage, so every
  test costs about the same.
- **Our resolution is about 10× coarser than Stockfish's.** A 2 pp window is about 14 Elo. Stockfish's
  <0, 2> nElo window at a 50% draw rate is about 1.4 logistic Elo.
- **Effects like MAGPIE's KLV3 leaves** (+0.44 points/game, about +1 Elo; magpie.md) cannot be settled by SPRT
  on this machine. For those, use spread per pair as a secondary statistic and S0 proxies. Otherwise, borrow
  compute.
- **Fixed-iteration budgets for S1/S2,** so a pair replays exactly. Macondo removes the per-turn deadline in
  pairs mode for this reason (reuse-check.md). Equal wall-clock time only at S3, where the engines differ in
  speed and speed is the point.
- **Position books** are the analogue of opening books:
  - start from positions drawn from real games, or from a phase the change affects (for example the bag-2
    positions Macondo used in #568);
  - bias towards positions where the bag is less decisive, to raise the share of informative pairs;
  - measure that share before adopting a book.
- **Non-regression for every simplification,** including removing features from our own net.

### What the referee (Macondo autoplay) still lacks

[reuse-check.md](../harness/reuse-check.md) ("Paired bags", "Gaps") already lists:

- no SPRT stopping;
- no clock, and timed pairs no longer replay exactly;
- a new process every turn;
- engines' own equity and win estimates not logged.

What this study adds:

- **A pentanomial tally.** `games-*.txt` has `pair` and `divergent` columns, and `autoanalyze` gives a sign-flip
  test and a paired t-test. Nothing counts the five pair outcomes or reports a pentanomial σ.
- **The informative-pair share.** Nothing reports the share of 2–0 / 0–2 pairs, which sets test cost.
- **Non-regression mode and an STC→LTC queue.** These are process, not code. Fishtest's worker pool and results
  database have no counterpart; at our scale a script and a TSV of runs would do.
